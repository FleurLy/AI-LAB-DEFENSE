## Risk explanation

The strongest indicators are failed SPF, DKIM, and DMARC authentication; a mismatch between the claimed constantcontact.com sender and the secure\-auth\-login.top Reply\-To domain; and language asking the recipient to log in. The message also contains payment\-related persuasion and unresolved template placeholders. Mild urgency and authority cues are present, but there is no secrecy request, explicit threat, attachment, or QR code. The Return\-Path matches the claimed sender domain, which is benign evidence, but it does not resolve the authentication failures. No usable URL was extracted, so the actual login destination could not be assessed from the supplied evidence.

## Recommended actions

- Do not provide credentials, reply to the message, or use any login or payment prompt contained in it.
- Verify the account independently by navigating to the service through a known official website or trusted application—not through the message.
- Report the message through your organization’s phishing\-reporting process, then quarantine or delete it according to policy.
- If credentials were already entered, change the password immediately using the official service, revoke active sessions where available, enable multifactor authentication, and notify the appropriate security team.
- If payment information was already provided, contact the relevant financial institution or payment provider and your security team promptly.
